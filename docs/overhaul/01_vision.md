# Vision: what it should feel like

Every phase is judged against this page. If a feature works but does not move
the game toward this, it is not done.

## One sentence

A quiet, slow, dangerous town that you come to know like a home, where every
life leaves something for the next one to find, use or bury.

## The four pillars (from the design document)

1. **Every action has weight.** Noise, light and blood draw the dead. What you
   take is gone for good. No choice is free.
2. **Slow burn.** The town gets worse whether or not you act: taps die, power
   fails, food rots, the dead gather, winter comes.
3. **Stories that happen.** Traits, weather, other people and earlier lives
   collide into things nobody scripted.
4. **The town remembers.** One fixed town per save. Graves, packs, boarded
   doors, burned streets, emptied shelves, a half-fuelled gate.

## How a moment should feel

- **Quiet first.** Most minutes are walking, listening, opening a cupboard.
  The game is calm enough that a door banging two houses away is an event.
- **You hear it before you see it.** Sound is the main sense. Every threat has
  a sound that arrives before it does, and every action of yours has a sound
  that carries.
- **Fights are a tax.** One of the dead is a problem you can solve. Three is a
  decision. Six is a mistake you made earlier. Nobody "clears" a street.
- **Hands, not menus.** Things are done in the world: push the wardrobe
  against the door, pry the board off, fill the bottle at the tap. Panels are
  for looking, not for doing.
- **The body tells you.** Breath, heartbeat, a shaking hand, a narrowed view.
  Numbers stay off the screen where a sign in the world can carry them.
- **Loss lands.** When a character dies the screen says what they leave
  behind, and the next character can walk to it.

## The arc of one life

| When | What it is about | What makes it turn |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Home. Search it, fill bottles, board the doors. | Night 1: two callers at the door. |
| Days 2 and 3 | Stock up; the neighbourhood. | Taps die at 03:00 on day 3. |
| Days 4 to 7 | The first week: a real base, a water source, the first trips into other districts. | Day 5: the power fails. Night 7: the first horde. |
| Days 8 to 15 | Reach: the far districts, other people, the campaign leads. | Supplies near home are gone. Weather turns. |
| Day 16 on | Endurance: growing food, the mind wearing down, seasons. | Summer heat, then autumn rain, then winter. |

A good first life lasts two to four days. A good tenth life lasts two weeks.
Dying on day 3 should feel like a story, not a waste: the next life starts
with a boarded house and a pack to fetch.

## The arc of one save

- **Lives 1 to 3:** learning the town. Graves near home. The first risen.
- **Lives 4 to 10:** a base that outlasts its builders. A notebook passed down
  through packs. Skills that echo in the bloodline. The first clue about the
  gate.
- **Later:** the near districts are picked clean and mostly graves. The work
  moves to the far side of town. Someone gets out, or the town runs dry.

A save has a natural end: the town empties. That is allowed. Nothing restocks.

## Tone

Grim, plain, humane. No gore for its own sake, no jokes, no heroics. The
warmth comes from small things: a note on a fridge, a bed that was made, a
stranger who shares a can. The three tone presets shift numbers and colour;
they do not change this voice.

## What "better" means for each area

- **Gameplay:** more decisions per minute that have two reasonable answers.
- **Feel:** every action has a wind-up, a contact and an aftermath you can see
  and hear.
- **Vibes:** weather, light and sound agree with each other and with the
  rules. If it looks cold, it is cold.
- **Simulation:** systems read each other. Rain quiets footsteps, puts out
  fires, fills collectors, chills you and washes blood away.
- **Back end:** content in data files, rules in small tested modules, a bot
  that can play a week overnight so numbers come from runs, not guesses.

## Things the game must never do

- Restock loot, respawn a cleared container, or heal the town.
- Kill the player with something they could not have seen or heard coming.
- Hide a rule the player needs. Hidden *recipes* and *clues* are fine; hidden
  *rules* are not.
- Stop the world for a tutorial. Teaching happens through the day plan, the
  prompt line and the first night.
